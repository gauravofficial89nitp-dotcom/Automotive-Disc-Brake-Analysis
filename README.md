# Automotive Disc Brake Analysis Using SolidWorks and ANSYS

## Project Overview

This project focuses on the CAD modelling and finite element analysis of an automotive disc brake to investigate its thermal and structural behaviour under specified operating conditions. The disc brake models were developed in SolidWorks and analysed using ANSYS to evaluate temperature distribution, equivalent (von Mises) stress, and total deformation.

The project includes multiple disc geometry configurations, labelled Model 1 to Model 5, along with thermal and structural analysis results. The objective is to examine the simulated response of the disc brake, identify regions with higher stress or temperature, and understand how geometry and loading conditions influence its performance.

## Objectives

- Develop 3D automotive disc brake geometries using SolidWorks.
- Prepare disc models for finite element analysis in ANSYS.
- Apply appropriate thermal and structural boundary conditions.
- Generate a finite element mesh for numerical analysis.
- Evaluate temperature distribution across the disc during the specified thermal loading.
- Analyse von Mises stress and total deformation under the defined structural conditions.
- Compare the simulation contours of different disc model configurations.

## Tools and Technologies

- **SolidWorks:** 3D CAD modelling of automotive disc brake geometries.
- **ANSYS Mechanical:** Thermal and static structural simulation.
- **Finite Element Analysis (FEA):** Numerical investigation of thermal and structural behaviour.
- **Meshing:** Discretization of the disc geometry into finite elements.
- **CAD/CAE:** Integration of computer-aided design and engineering analysis.
- **Post-Processing:** Interpretation of temperature, stress, and deformation contours.

## Project Methodology

### 1. 3D CAD Modelling

The automotive disc brake geometries were developed in SolidWorks. The attached project files contain an original disc model and five model configurations with variations in their geometric features, including the arrangement of slots and openings.

These geometries were prepared for subsequent thermal and structural analysis.

### 2. Geometry Preparation

The disc models were imported or prepared for analysis in ANSYS. The geometry was checked to ensure that it could be used for meshing, boundary-condition assignment, and numerical simulation.

### 3. Meshing

The disc geometry was divided into finite elements to enable numerical computation. The attached project files include a mesh visualization of the disc.

Mesh quality and element distribution are important for resolving local temperature gradients, stress concentrations, and deformation around holes, slots, and other geometric features.

### 4. Thermal Analysis

Thermal analysis was conducted to investigate the temperature distribution across the disc under the specified thermal boundary conditions.

The project includes thermal boundary-condition and temperature-contour images for the disc configurations.

The analysis focuses on:

- Temperature distribution across the disc surface.
- Differences between higher- and lower-temperature regions.
- Thermal behaviour around the disc's slots, holes, and surrounding regions.
- Comparison of temperature contours across the different configurations.

These results help explain how heat is distributed throughout the disc under the modelled thermal conditions.

### 5. Static Structural Analysis

Static structural analysis was performed to examine the mechanical response of the disc under the specified structural boundary conditions.

The project includes equivalent (von Mises) stress contours and total deformation results for the different configurations.

The main parameters investigated are:

- **Equivalent (von Mises) Stress:** Used to identify regions of higher equivalent stress and assess the structural response under the applied loading.
- **Total Deformation:** Represents the resultant displacement of the disc under the specified structural conditions.
- **Stress Concentration:** Helps identify localized regions requiring further investigation, particularly around holes, slots, and changes in geometry.

The stress and deformation contours provide a basis for comparing the structural response of the disc models.

### 6. Comparative Analysis

The project contains simulation outputs for Model 1 to Model 5, along with results labelled as the actual model in the analysis folders.

The available thermal, stress, and deformation plots can be compared to investigate differences in the simulated behaviour of the configurations.

A quantitative ranking of the models requires extracting their actual maximum temperature, maximum von Mises stress, and maximum deformation values under comparable material properties, loads, boundary conditions, and solver settings.

## Results and Discussion

### Thermal Results

The thermal analysis provides temperature contours for the different disc configurations. These plots show the spatial variation of temperature under the specified thermal conditions.

The results can be used to identify regions with relatively higher temperatures and examine the thermal response associated with different geometries.

### Structural Results

The von Mises stress contours illustrate the distribution of equivalent stress across the disc. The deformation plots show the predicted displacement pattern under the applied structural conditions.

Regions with elevated stress or deformation warrant further investigation to understand their significance for structural performance.

### Model Comparison

Comparing Models 1–5 helps investigate the effect of geometric variations on the simulated thermal and structural response. The final assessment should be based on the numerical values displayed in ANSYS, rather than contour colours alone.

**Note:** Exact maximum temperature, maximum stress, and deformation values have not been included here because they should be extracted and verified from the individual ANSYS result plots.

## Limitations and Future Scope

The reliability of the simulation results depends on the accuracy of the geometry, material properties, mesh, loads, thermal boundary conditions, and structural constraints.

Further work could include mesh-convergence studies, quantitative comparison of all five configurations, transient thermal analysis, coupled thermal-structural analysis, and experimental validation. These extensions would help establish a more complete understanding of disc brake performance under realistic braking conditions.

## Conclusion

This project combines SolidWorks-based 3D modelling with ANSYS thermal and static structural analysis to investigate the behaviour of automotive disc brake geometries. Temperature distribution, equivalent stress, and total deformation are examined across multiple model configurations.

The project demonstrates a practical CAD/CAE workflow for evaluating mechanical components and interpreting finite element simulation results to support engineering design assessment.
